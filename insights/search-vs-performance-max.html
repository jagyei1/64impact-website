Search vs Performance Max: which one actually fits your business

A clear-eyed look at when automation helps and when it hides exactly where your budget is going.

Jude Agyei · Founder, 64impact · Google Ads

Performance Max has become Google's default recommendation for most advertisers, and it can genuinely work well. But it isn't automatically the right choice, and understanding the trade-off matters more than following the platform's suggestion.

What Search campaigns give you

Standard Search campaigns give you visibility and control: which keywords you're bidding on, what the ad copy says, which landing page each search term goes to, and detailed reporting on exactly which search terms are converting. That transparency makes it possible to continuously refine an account based on real evidence.

What Performance Max gives you

Performance Max automates targeting and placement across Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail and more from a single campaign, using machine learning to find converting audiences. It can access inventory and signals that a manually managed Search campaign can't, and for the right business, that reach genuinely drives results a Search-only strategy wouldn't reach.

The trade-off: less visibility

The cost of that automation is reduced reporting. Performance Max gives far less detail on which specific searches, placements or audiences are actually driving results, which makes it harder to know why performance changed or where to intervene when something isn't working. For a business that needs to understand exactly where its budget is going, that's a real limitation, not a minor inconvenience.

When PMax tends to help

Ecommerce businesses with a broad product catalogue and clear purchase-conversion signals tend to see PMax perform well, because the algorithm has plenty of data to learn from and a straightforward outcome to optimise toward.

When PMax tends to hide problems

Businesses with high-value, considered purchases, longer sales cycles, or a need for tight control over messaging and targeting often do better with Search campaigns run alongside, or instead of, Performance Max. When the buying decision is complex, the reduced visibility of PMax can mask exactly the kind of issue that needs to be caught early.

The practical answer

For most businesses, the right approach isn't choosing one exclusively. It's understanding what each campaign type is good at, and structuring the account so Search handles what needs control and transparency, while PMax is given clear guardrails rather than the entire budget.
